Flipbook

Your hand is the frame rate.

A skin for Video.js 10. The timeline is the book’s fore-edge. Drag along it and the film moves under your hand, forwards and back, as fast as you go. The first moving pictures were flipbooks, and this player is one.

Features
Details

Essential is the player alone; the full book adds its extras, each loaded the first time it’s used. Details are trial touches on the controls: switch each off to see the control as it was. On a touch screen fullscreen and volume keep their familiar glyphs.

Try it

What the book does that you won’t find by looking. The keys work as soon as the page is open, without clicking the player first. Those marked Book are the book’s extras: on with Full book, off with Essential.

Drag
Drag along the fore-edge. Part of the Book Point at the timeline and the picture steps back while the pages fan out around that moment. Drag, and they riffle past. Move up, away from the edge, for finer control. With Essential, a single card shows the frame, its time and its chapter.
Flick
Flick to throw the pages. Part of the Book Let go mid-drag and they fly on, slowing as they go; near the start of a chapter, they settle on it.
T
Contents. Part of the Book The film’s table of contents: each chapter with its frame, a dotted leader and the time it starts, the one playing marked in red. Your bookmarks follow.
B [ ]
Bookmark ribbons. Part of the Book B drops a red ribbon between the pages at the moment playing; [ and ] go to the previous and next one. They stay in this browser.
Corner
The corner, when paused. Part of the Book The page’s top corner lifts on the next chapter’s first frame. Pull it, or click it, to turn there.
P
The Muybridge plate. Part of the Book The twelve frames around the paused moment, laid out four by three, numbered and timed, as Muybridge printed a horse’s gallop. Each frame takes you there.
N
Next chapter. Turns the page to the next chapter at any time.
I
Picture-in-picture. Part of the Book In Chrome and Edge the whole book goes into the window: fore-edge, chapter tabs, captions and keys. Its place on the page turns over to a waiting page that keeps following the film. With Essential, the same key opens the browser’s own video window.
⇧ C
Caption style. Size, backing, colour and position, each applied at once, with a preview line set on the frame you’re watching.
?
Every shortcut. All the keys and gestures on one card.
Scroll
Scroll on while it plays. Part of the Book The book docks in the corner as a small copy of itself, and its place here stays held, so nothing on the page moves.

How it holds up

Weights

The core carries Video.js 10 itself: the controls, the fore-edge with its tabs and preview card, captions and every accessibility fix. The book’s extras are separate chunks, each fetched the first time it’s used, or while the page is idle. Essential never fetches them. Either way the first paint is the same: the extras arrive afterwards and move nothing.

Over bright frames and dark ones

The controls sit on their own cover board, never on the picture, so they read the same over a white sky as over a black stage. Captions sit on an ink backing that keeps them above 7:1 over a white frame, and the focus ring is registration red on an ink plate of its own.

Sized for a thumb

Keys, chapter tabs and ribbons are drawn to a 44 px target, on a phone as on a desktop. With less room, the keys’ names fold away and their keycaps stay; below 480 px, a Shortcuts key opens the card of keys.

A keyboard is enough

Tab reaches every control in the order you see it, each with the same red ring. The chapter tabs, the ribbons and the plate’s frames are one stop each, with the arrow keys inside. The contents, the plate and the cards close with Escape and hand focus back to where it was.

Still, when asked

With reduced motion, nothing travels. The cover goes instead of turning, the corner lies folded instead of lifting, and the plate and the contents simply appear. Every change still shows; only the travel is gone.

Picture-in-picture keeps the book

In the window, keys still reach the player and the chapters stay a click away. In Safari, which has no document window, the same key opens the browser’s own video window; Firefox has no picture-in-picture API and offers its own button on the video instead, so the key steps aside there.
